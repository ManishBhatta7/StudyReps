Have you ever seen a tightrope walker perfectly balanced, or a huge bridge standing absolutely still for decades? How does a boomerang fly in a steady arc without tumbling randomly? They're all fascinating examples of something called *equilibrium*!

So, what exactly is equilibrium? It’s when a body is perfectly *balanced*. This happens when two crucial conditions are met: first, the *net force* acting on it is zero, meaning all pushes and pulls cancel each other out. And second, the *net moment*, or turning effect, is also zero – so no twisting or rotating happens.

We have two main types. If a body is completely *at rest* and stays at rest, like a book on your table, that’s called *static equilibrium*. All forces and moments are perfectly balanced, and nothing is moving. But equilibrium isn't just about being still! If a body is moving at a *constant velocity* – meaning a steady speed in a straight line – it's in *dynamic equilibrium*. Think of a car cruising steadily on a straight highway. No acceleration, so the net force is still zero!

And for those turning effects, we use the *Principle of Moments*. It states that for a body to be balanced rotationally, the total turning effect *clockwise* must perfectly equal the total turning effect *anticlockwise*. It’s how a see-saw works!

Take our tightrope walker again. To stay balanced, they need zero net force pulling them down or sideways, and zero net moment trying to twist them off the rope. Their arms outstretched help create tiny opposing moments to correct any slight lean, ensuring they maintain *static equilibrium*.

So, remember: equilibrium means *net force* and *net moment* are both zero. It can be *static* if an object is at rest, or *dynamic* if it's moving at a constant velocity. It's all about perfect balance!
