Ever wondered what makes a soccer ball fly after a kick, or why a car eventually stops even without hitting the brakes?

The answer to all these is *force*! Simply put, a force is a *push* or a *pull*. It's a physical cause that can *change the state of rest or motion* of an object. This means if something is still, a force can make it move, and if it's moving, a force can make it stop, speed up, slow down, or even change direction. The standard international unit for measuring force is the *Newton*, represented by a capital N. We also have a smaller unit called *Dyne* in the CGS system. One Newton is equal to *one hundred thousand Dyne*, or 10 to the power of 5 Dyne! Forces can be broadly divided into two main types. First, we have *contact forces*, which require two objects to physically touch. Think about *friction*, which slows things down, or *tension* when you pull on a rope, or the *normal force* supporting a book on a table. Second, there are *non-contact forces* that act from a distance, without any physical touch. The most famous one is *gravity*, which pulls everything towards the Earth. Then there's the *magnetic force* between magnets, and *electric force*, like when static electricity makes your hair stand on end.

Let's think about throwing a ball. When you throw it, you exert a *contact force* on it, making it move. As it flies, air resistance (a type of *friction*) acts as another contact force trying to slow it down. But what pulls it back to the ground? That's *gravity*, a powerful *non-contact force*. So, in one simple action, you see both types of forces at play!

So, remember, a *force* is a push or pull that changes motion, measured in *Newtons*, and can be either a *contact force* requiring touch, or a *non-contact force* acting from a distance.
